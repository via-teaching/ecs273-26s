If you are wondering whether Bank of America at around US$52.05 still offers value, it helps to break the story into what the price has done and what the fundamentals suggest.
The stock has had mixed momentum recently, with a 3.5% decline over the last 7 days, a 6.8% gain over the past 30 days, a 7.0% year to date decline, and a 34.1% return over the last year, plus a 92.2% return over 3 years and 45.0% over 5 years.
Recent coverage has focused on Bank of America as one of the major US banks investors watch closely for signals about credit conditions, loan demand, and consumer activity. Headlines often highlight how large banks are positioned for changes in interest rates and regulation, which helps explain why sentiment around Bank of America can shift quickly.
Simply Wall St currently gives Bank of America a valuation score of 5 out of 6 , based on six separate checks of whether the shares trade below various estimates of fair value. The Excess Returns model looks at how effectively Bank of America turns its equity base into profits above the return that shareholders require. Instead of focusing on cash flows, it compares the bank’s return on equity to its cost of equity and then capitalizes those “excess” profits into an intrinsic value per share.
For Bank of America, book value is estimated at $38.66 per share, with a stable book value projection of $43.25 per share, based on weighted future book value estimates from 14 analysts. Stable EPS is $5.19 per share, sourced from weighted future return on equity estimates from the same analyst group. The average return on equity is 12.01%, while the cost of equity is $3.84 per share, leaving an excess return of $1.35 per share.
Using these inputs, the Excess Returns model points to an intrinsic value of about $68.05 per share. At a current share price around $52.05, this implies the shares trade at roughly a 23.5% discount. On this framework, the stock screens as undervalued.

For a profitable bank like Bank of America, the P/E ratio is a straightforward way to see what investors are currently willing to pay for each dollar of earnings. It connects directly to your expectations about how reliable those earnings are and how they might evolve over time.
In general, higher expected growth and lower perceived risk can justify a higher P/E, while slower expected growth or higher risk usually means a lower, more cautious multiple. Bank of America currently trades on a P/E of 12.27x. That sits slightly above the Banks industry average of 11.71x and below the peer group average of 13.47x.
Simply Wall St’s Fair Ratio for Bank of America is 21.22x. This Fair Ratio is a proprietary estimate of what P/E could be reasonable for the company, taking into account factors such as earnings growth, profitability, risk profile, market cap and its Banks industry context. Because it incorporates these company specific inputs, it can be more informative than a simple comparison with peers or the broad industry. Comparing the Fair Ratio of 21.22x to the current P/E of 12.27x suggests the shares trade below that model based reference point.
