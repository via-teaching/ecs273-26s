Analysts have lifted the fair value estimate for Johnson & Johnson from US$241.71 to US$252.42, while price targets across recent research now span from around US$250 up to US$280. That shift reflects ongoing debate about how much growth key drugs like Tremfya and late stage assets can support, with some firms leaning more optimistic and others staying Neutral even as they raise targets. As you read on, you will see how to interpret these moves and track the evolving story around Johnson & Johnson.

Several firms, including UBS, HSBC, Citi, Barclays, JPMorgan, BofA, Morgan Stanley, RBC Capital and Stifel, have raised Johnson & Johnson price targets, pointing to updated views on key products such as Tremfya, Tecvayli, Inlexzo and Darzalex, as well as late stage pipeline assets.
UBS and HSBC both moved targets to US$280, while Citi, Barclays and JPMorgan also lifted their levels. This indicates that a number of large houses see room for the shares to support higher valuation ranges than before.
BofA, Citi and JPMorgan commentary links target changes to adjustments in revenue expectations or product assumptions. This gives you specific levers to watch, such as uptake and treatment duration in immunology and oncology franchises.
Not all firms shifted to an outright positive stance, with JPMorgan and BofA maintaining Neutral ratings even as they raised price targets. This signals ongoing questions around execution, competitive intensity and how fully current expectations already reflect the pipeline.
The spread in targets, from around US$250 at JPMorgan and BofA to US$280 at UBS and HSBC, highlights that analysts are not aligned on the risk reward trade off. You should pay close attention to differences in assumptions on product growth and regulatory or clinical milestones.

A California judge overturned a US$950m punitive damages award in a talc trial, finding plaintiffs did not sufficiently prove Johnson & Johnson acted with malice or hid required information.
A Canada-wide class action over talc-based Baby Powder was certified, alleging inadequate warnings on potential ovarian cancer risk and misleading safety claims. The court has not yet ruled on the merits.
A Philadelphia jury awarded US$250,000 in compensatory and punitive damages in an ovarian cancer case tied to talc products. This has been described as the first of several talc trials scheduled for 2026.
Bloomberg reported that Johnson & Johnson is preparing a possible sale of its DePuy Synthes orthopedics unit. Sources suggest the unit could be valued at more than US$20b and attract both private equity and industry buyers.
