Jet2’s analyst narrative has shifted again, with fair value trimmed from £16.61 to £16.34 and recent Street price targets moving in step, such as JPMorgan’s cut from £14.50 to £14.00 and Morgan Stanley’s move from £14.50 to £13.25. These changes reflect banks revisiting their models while largely keeping neutral stances. This leaves you with a cluster of targets around £13.25 to £14.00 to measure against your own view. JPMorgan, Deutsche Bank and RBC Capital are all still publishing research on Jet2, which indicates the stock remains firmly on the radar of large global institutions.
JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley both maintain neutral-style ratings, which signals that even after trimming numbers they still see a case for holding the shares rather than stepping away entirely.
JPMorgan, Deutsche Bank and RBC Capital have each lowered price targets in recent months, which points to more cautious modeling on earnings power and return potential.
Morgan Stanley’s move from £14.50 to £13.25 and JPMorgan’s cut to £14.00 bring targets into a tighter band, which may suggest less perceived room for upside against current trading levels in their base cases.

This £439m EBIT figure is framed as the average view among covering analysts for the 2026 financial year, which you can compare with your own assumptions on demand, costs and capacity.

The future P/E multiple is revised from 8.64x to 8.50x.
