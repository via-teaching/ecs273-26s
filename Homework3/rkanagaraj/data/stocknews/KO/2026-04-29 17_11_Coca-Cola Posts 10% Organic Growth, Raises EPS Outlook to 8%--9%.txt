Coca-Cola (NYSE:KO) delivered a stronger-than-expected quarter, with organic revenue rising 10%, marking its best performance in five quarters and coming in ahead of analyst estimates, while adjusted earnings per share also topped expectations. The market response followed quickly, with shares rising as much as 5.8%, extending a roughly 8% gain this year compared with about a 5% increase in the S&P 500 Index. The performance could suggest that Coca-Cola's evolving pricing and packaging strategy is beginning to resonate as consumer spending patterns continue to diverge.

Management has been leaning into a dual-track approach, offering smaller, more affordable packaging for price-sensitive consumers while also introducing premium products for those with greater spending power. In North America, mini can volumes grew at a high-single-digit percentage after the company expanded single-serve offerings in convenience stores, while in the UK, a larger Superfan can tied to the English Premier League contributed to sales momentum. Chief Executive Officer Henrique Braun indicated that lower-income consumers remain under pressure, which has led the company to increase its focus on affordability options.
Looking ahead, Coca-Cola raised its full-year comparable earnings per share growth outlook to 8% to 9%, up from 7% to 8%, while maintaining its organic revenue growth forecast of 4% to 5%. Management noted that the effects of the war with Iran have so far been manageable, though conditions remain fluid and could shift depending on developments in the Middle East, with some volume declines already seen in March. Within the product mix, soda sales rose 2%, supported by a 13% increase in Coca-Cola Zero Sugar and a 6% gain in Diet Coke, pointing to continued momentum in lower-sugar alternatives.
