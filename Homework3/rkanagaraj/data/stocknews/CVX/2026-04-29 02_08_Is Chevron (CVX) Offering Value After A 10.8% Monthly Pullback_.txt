If you are wondering whether Chevron's current share price reflects its true worth, you are not alone. This article focuses squarely on what you are paying versus what you may be getting.
Chevron recently closed at US$188.36, with a 1.3% return over the last 7 days, a 10.8% decline over 30 days, and gains of 20.8% year to date and 41.3% over the last year.
These moves sit against a backdrop of ongoing attention on large integrated energy companies, with investors regularly weighing factors such as commodity prices, capital spending plans, and shareholder return policies. Together, these themes often shape how the market reacts to Chevron's stock price over shorter and longer periods.

A Discounted Cash Flow, or DCF, model looks at the cash Chevron is expected to generate in the future and then discounts those projected cash flows back into a single value in today’s dollars.
For Chevron, the latest twelve month Free Cash Flow is about $18.0b. Using a 2 Stage Free Cash Flow to Equity model, analysts and extrapolations feed in projected Free Cash Flow out to 2035, with Simply Wall St extending estimates beyond the initial analyst horizon. By 2030, the forecast Free Cash Flow used in the model is $30.3b, with later years also expressed in tens of billions of dollars, all in $.
When all those future cash flows are discounted back and summed, the model arrives at an intrinsic value of about $358.56 per share. Compared with the recent share price of $188.36, this DCF output suggests Chevron trades at a 47.5% discount, which implies the stock screens as materially undervalued on this approach.
Result: UNDERVALUED

For a profitable company like Chevron, the P/E ratio is a useful way to relate what you are paying for the stock to the earnings the business is currently generating. Investors typically accept a higher or lower P/E based on what they expect for future earnings growth and how much risk they see in those earnings.
Chevron trades on a P/E of 30.5x, compared with an Oil and Gas industry average of 14.9x and a peer group average of 24.6x. On the surface, that points to a richer valuation than both the wider industry and closer peers.
Simply Wall St's Fair Ratio for Chevron is 30.9x. This is a proprietary estimate of what Chevron's P/E might be given its earnings growth profile, profit margins, industry, market value and risk characteristics. Because it blends these company specific factors, the Fair Ratio can be more informative than a simple comparison with broad industry or peer averages.
Set against this Fair Ratio, Chevron's actual P/E of 30.5x sits slightly below, which indicates the shares screen as somewhat undervalued on this metric.
