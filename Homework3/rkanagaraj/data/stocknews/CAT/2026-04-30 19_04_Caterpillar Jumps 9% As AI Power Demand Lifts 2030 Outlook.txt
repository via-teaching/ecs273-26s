Caterpillar (NYSE:CAT) shares moved sharply higher after the company delivered a stronger-than-expected first quarter and raised its long-term revenue outlook, giving investors a fresh signal that demand for construction machinery and power-generation equipment could remain stronger than previously expected. Shares rose about 9% in morning trading on Thursday in New York after the company said it now expects sales to increase at an annual rate of 6% to 9% through 2030, above its previous 5% to 7% forecast. Caterpillar reported adjusted per-share profit of $5.54 for the first quarter, up from $4.25 a year earlier and ahead of the $4.63 average estimate compiled by Bloomberg. The company also said revenue from its machinery, power and energy unit topped Wall Streets estimates, while its construction industries unit saw sales rise 38% as dealers stocked up on equipment.

A major driver of the new investor enthusiasm appears to be Caterpillars power and energy business, which includes generators, engines and gas turbines used in industrial facilities and large-scale computing centers. Chief Executive Officer Joe Creed told analysts that the backlog for some power-generation equipment has more than tripled since 2024, with some orders extending well into 2028 as customers commit to longer-term machinery purchases. Creed also said the company is seeing higher demand for prime power applications, which could lead to long-term service opportunities. That backdrop may connect Caterpillar more directly to the AI infrastructure buildout, as data centers require large amounts of electricity and major technology companies continue to spend heavily on capacity. Meta Platforms (NASDAQ:META) and Amazon.com (NASDAQ:AMZN) both reported higher-than-expected capital expenditures on Wednesday, while SLC Managements Dec Mullarkey said Caterpillar is benefiting from the AI buildout and could remain an essential player as related capex continues.
The quarter also showed that Caterpillars strength was not limited to one business line, with the company reporting a record order backlog and stronger demand across construction and power-related equipment. Oppenheimer & Co. analyst Kristen Owen said the new growth targets show the durability of demand in Caterpillars power and energy unit, reinforcing the idea that the companys longer-term outlook could be supported by more than a short-cycle rebound. Still, the earnings story came with a cost warning. Caterpillar said higher tariff costs weighed on manufacturing expenses across all its units, and the company expects $2.2 billion to $2.4 billion in tariff-related costs for the full year.
