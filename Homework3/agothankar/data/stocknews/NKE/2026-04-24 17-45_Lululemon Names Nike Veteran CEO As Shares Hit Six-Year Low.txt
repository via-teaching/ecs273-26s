Title: Lululemon Names Nike Veteran CEO As Shares Hit Six-Year Low
Date: 2026-04-24 17:45
URL: https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/lululemon-names-nike-veteran-ceo-174525054.html?.tsrc=rss

This article first appeared onGuruFocus. Lululemon Athletica (NASDAQ:LULU) hasmovedto appoint former Nike (NYSE:NKE) executive Heidi O'Neill as chief executive officer following the departure of Calvin McDonald in December, but the decision may be landing uneasily with key stakeholders and investors. The announcement comes against the backdrop of activist Elliott Investment Management's $1 billion stake and its support for an alternative candidate, Jane Nielsen, while founder Chip Wilson has been calling for broader board changes before a leadership decision. Shares reacted sharply, falling to a six-year low, suggesting the market is still weighing whether this leadership transition can address the company's ongoing challenges. Warning! O'Neill brings decades of experience from Nike, where she held senior roles across consumer, product, and brand operations, and was previously considered a potential CEO candidate there. That background could position her to tackle Lululemon's operational and brand issues, although questions may remain around how directly her experience translates to the company's current needs. Lululemon is facing intensifying competition from newer entrants such as Alo Yoga and Vuori, shifting consumer preferences toward more tailored styles, and recent product missteps including quality concerns tied to its Get Low leggings. Her start date is set for September, which could leave a period of uncertainty as the company navigates these pressures. The reaction from investors may also reflect what was not chosen, with Nielsen viewed by some as a candidate with strong operational discipline following her tenure at Ralph Lauren. Her experience in refining brand positioning and focusing on core product lines could have offered a different path to stabilizing margins, though it may not have addressed product innovation challenges. Instead, the board's decision introduces a potential comparison that could persist, particularly given the diverging share performance between Ralph Lauren and Nike in recent years. At the same time, Wilson's continued push for additional board changes suggests governance tensions could remain in focus, adding another layer of complexity as Lululemon works to rebuild momentum.
