Alphabet announces plans to invest up to $40b in Anthropic, deepening their AI partnership. As part of the deal, Anthropic will use Google Cloud as its primary AI infrastructure provider. The agreement includes long term commitments around cloud capacity and access to Alphabet’s TPU chips. Alphabet, listed as NasdaqGS:GOOGL, is tying this large AI commitment to a share price of $344.4. The stock has returned 18.4% over the past 30 days and 9.3% year to date, with a 1 year return of 113.4%. Over 3 years and 5 years, returns of 223.5% and 195.1% respectively show how closely investors have been watching the company’s AI moves. For investors, this Anthropic deal relates directly to how Alphabet aims to position its TPU chip ecosystem and Google Cloud in core AI workloads. The size and structure of the commitment may influence how capital flows into AI infrastructure, model providers, and competing cloud platforms, so it is an area to watch closely as details and execution progress. The planned investment of up to US$40b in Anthropic gives Alphabet more than just financial exposure to one AI lab; it helps lock in cloud and chip demand that lines up with its heavy AI capital spending. Anthropic agreeing to use Google Cloud as its primary AI infrastructure provider and committing to long term TPU usage ties directly into Alphabet’s push to scale proprietary AI hardware alongside services. For readers comparing Alphabet with Microsoft Azure and Amazon Web Services, this looks like an attempt to secure a flagship, model-first customer that can showcase Google’s TPUs as an alternative to Nvidia GPUs. The Anthropic agreement supports the narrative that rising AI adoption can feed Google Cloud’s backlog and help Alphabet spread the cost of large AI infrastructure projects across more usage. The size of the commitment increases execution risk around very high capital expenditure, which has been highlighted as a concern if revenue growth does not keep pace. The performance based structure of the extra US$30b and the specifics of TPU monetization are not fully reflected in the broad narrative about AI driven growth and may change how investors think about future cash flows from cloud and chips.
