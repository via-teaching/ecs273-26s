McDonald's (NYSE:MCD) is rolling out six new specialty beverages across all U.S. locations starting May 6.
The lineup includes three fruit focused Refreshers and three crafted sodas, paired with a limited edition designer drink carrier.
The company plans to phase out self serve soda machines in U.S. restaurants by 2032, shifting drink preparation behind the counter.
For a company built around burgers, fries and core beverages, this move signals a stronger push into higher variety drink offerings within McDonald's existing quick service model. The broader restaurant industry has been paying more attention to specialty drinks as a way to attract visits throughout the day, not just at traditional meal times.
For you as an investor, these changes provide more detail on how McDonald's is trying to increase customer engagement and refine in store operations. The combination of a broader beverage menu and the move away from self serve soda stations could influence order patterns, labor needs and the overall guest experience in the years ahead.

This beverage push points to McDonald's trying to turn drinks into a more consistent traffic driver across the day, not just an add on to meals. By leaning into Refreshers and crafted sodas, the company is moving closer to how Starbucks, Dunkin and some regional chains use beverages to anchor visit frequency. For you, the key question is whether this broader drink range can attract incremental visits rather than simply shifting spend away from existing menu items.
The expanded McCafé lineup aligns with the narrative focus on menu innovation and digital engagement. It gives customers more reasons to use the app, loyalty program and drive thru for drink specific trips.
Phasing out self serve soda machines could test customer patience during peak periods if operations are not set up efficiently. That would run against the aim of using technology and process changes to support better throughput.
The narrative highlights store growth, digital and value. This launch adds a fashion collaboration and higher variety beverages that may not be fully captured in existing assumptions around brand relevance and non food revenue mix.
