Investors are set to hear from Bank of America and Morgan Stanley on Wednesday after a string of big banks’ first-quarter earnings reports reflected solid U.S. consumer health. Results from JPMorgan Chase, Citigroup, and Wells Fargo showed that measures of consumer credit, such as net charge-offs and delinquencies, were steady in the first quarter, even as borrowers faced higher energy prices prompted by the U.S.-Iran war. Shares of Wells Fargo sunk 5.7% after the firm missed Wall Street's revenue and net interest income expectations, while JPMorgan's stock fell 0.8% and Citi's rose 2.6%.
