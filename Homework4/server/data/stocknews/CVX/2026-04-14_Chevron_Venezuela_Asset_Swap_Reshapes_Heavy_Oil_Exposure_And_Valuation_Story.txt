Chevron (NYSE:CVX) has agreed to increase its ownership in the Petroindependencia joint venture in Venezuela to 49%.
The company has also secured development rights in the Orinoco Oil Belt, one of the world's largest heavy oil reserves.
The deal is taking place as US sanctions on Venezuela are eased, allowing Chevron more operational flexibility in the country.
The agreement involves state oil company PDVSA and focuses on heavy crude production and long term development potential.
For Chevron, this move fits within its core upstream oil and gas business, with a focus on heavy crude resources. Venezuela's Orinoco Belt is widely recognized for its heavy oil deposits, and increased access there gives Chevron a larger footprint in an important supply region for global energy markets.
Investors watching NYSE:CVX may view this agreement as an indication of the company's willingness to commit capital and expertise to long-duration projects in higher-risk regions. The deal also introduces additional geopolitical and regulatory considerations into the investment narrative, which could affect how you evaluate Chevron's risk profile and portfolio balance over time.
