If you are wondering whether Microsoft is priced for opportunity or already fully valued, it helps to start with what the recent share price is really telling you.
The stock last closed at US$384.37, with returns of 3.1% over 7 days, a 2.8% decline over 30 days, an 18.7% decline year to date, but a small 0.1% decline over 1 year alongside gains of 36.2% over 3 years and 54.8% over 5 years.
Recent news has focused on Microsoft's role at the center of long term themes such as cloud computing and artificial intelligence, as well as its ongoing investments in expanding products and services. This backdrop helps frame why the share price has seen both shorter term pullbacks and longer term strength in returns.

A Discounted Cash Flow, or DCF, model takes estimates of a company’s future cash flows and discounts them back to today’s dollars to arrive at an implied intrinsic value per share.
For Microsoft, the model used is a 2 Stage Free Cash Flow to Equity approach, based on cash flow projections in US$. The latest twelve month free cash flow is about US$93.7b. Analyst estimates are provided for the next several years, and then Simply Wall St extrapolates beyond that, with projected free cash flow in 2035 of roughly US$285.8b.
These future cash flows are discounted to today using the model’s assumptions, which results in an estimated intrinsic value of about US$482.86 per share. Compared with the recent share price of US$384.37, the model implies Microsoft trades at roughly a 20.4% discount, so on this measure the stock screens as undervalued rather than fully priced.

For a profitable company like Microsoft, the P/E ratio is a useful way to think about value because it links the share price directly to the earnings you are paying for each share. A higher P/E typically reflects stronger growth expectations or lower perceived risk, while a lower P/E can point to more modest growth assumptions or higher risk.
Microsoft currently trades on a P/E ratio of 23.93x. That sits below both the Software industry average of about 28.05x and the peer average of 28.98x, which suggests the market is pricing Microsoft slightly more conservatively than many comparable names. To go a step further, Simply Wall St also estimates a proprietary “Fair Ratio” of 41.24x for Microsoft.
The Fair Ratio is designed to reflect the P/E multiple that might be reasonable given factors such as earnings growth, profit margins, industry, market cap and specific risks. Because it ties the multiple to company specific drivers instead of just broad peer groups, it can offer a more tailored view than a simple comparison to industry or peer averages. With Microsoft’s current P/E of 23.93x sitting below the Fair Ratio of 41.24x, this framework points to the shares screening as undervalued on this metric.
