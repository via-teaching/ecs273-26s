The Excess Returns model looks at how much value Goldman Sachs Group generates above the return that equity investors are assumed to require. It starts from the book value of the business and compares the returns on that equity to the cost of equity.

For Goldman Sachs Group, book value is estimated at $356.47 per share, with a stable book value of $402.19 per share based on weighted future book value estimates from 14 analysts. Stable EPS is estimated at $67.46 per share, sourced from weighted future return on equity estimates from 13 analysts. The average return on equity is 16.77% and the cost of equity is $37.03 per share, which results in an excess return of $30.42 per share.

Simply Wall St aggregates these excess returns over time and discounts them to today, producing an intrinsic value estimate of about $926.90 per share. Compared with the recent share price of around $863, this implies the stock is 6.9% undervalued, which is a relatively modest gap.

For a profitable company like Goldman Sachs Group, the P/E ratio is a useful shorthand for how much you are paying for each dollar of earnings. It helps you quickly compare what the market is willing to pay for this earnings stream versus other banks and the broader Capital Markets industry.

What counts as a “normal” or “fair” P/E depends on how the market views a company’s growth prospects and risk. Higher expected growth or lower perceived risk can justify a higher P/E, while lower growth or higher risk usually aligns with a lower multiple.

Goldman Sachs Group currently trades on a P/E of 16.29x. That is below the Capital Markets industry average of 36.13x and below the peer group average of 24.80x. Simply Wall St also calculates a Fair Ratio of 18.11x, which is the P/E that would typically be expected given factors like Goldman Sachs Group’s earnings profile, industry, profit margins, market cap and specific risks.

This Fair Ratio can be more informative than a simple peer or industry comparison because it is tailored to the company’s own fundamentals rather than broad group averages. With the current 16.29x P/E sitting below the 18.11x Fair Ratio, the shares appear moderately undervalued on this metric.
