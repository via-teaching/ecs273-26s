A Discounted Cash Flow model estimates what a company could be worth today by projecting its future cash flows and then discounting those back to a present value. It is essentially asking what all of Meta Platforms future cash flows are worth in today’s dollars.

For Meta Platforms, Simply Wall St uses a 2 Stage Free Cash Flow to Equity model. The latest twelve month free cash flow is about $61.98b. Analyst estimates, supplemented by Simply Wall St extrapolations beyond the usual 5 year window, point to projected free cash flow of $119.49b in 2030. There is a series of annual projections in between that are discounted back to today.

Bringing all of those projected cash flows into today’s terms results in an estimated intrinsic value of about $1,178.23 per share. Compared with the current share price of around $573, the model implies a 51.4% discount. This indicates that the shares are currently priced below this cash flow based estimate of value.

For profitable companies, the P/E ratio is a useful way to relate what you pay for each share to the earnings that each share currently generates. It gives a quick sense of how many years of current earnings the market is effectively pricing in.

What counts as a "normal" P/E depends on how the market views a company’s growth prospects and risk. Higher expected growth or lower perceived risk can justify a higher P/E, while lower growth or higher risk typically lines up with a lower P/E.

Meta Platforms currently trades on a P/E of 23.98x. This sits above the Interactive Media and Services industry average of 14.25x, but below the peer group average of 30.24x. To refine this, Simply Wall St calculates a proprietary “Fair Ratio” of 39.93x, which reflects factors such as earnings growth, profit margins, industry, market cap and risk profile.

This Fair Ratio is more tailored than a simple comparison with peers or the industry because it adjusts for Meta Platforms specific characteristics rather than treating all companies as if they deserve the same multiple. Compared with the current P/E of 23.98x, the Fair Ratio of 39.93x suggests the shares trade below this implied level.

Earlier we mentioned that there is an even better way to understand valuation, so this is where Narratives come in as a simple way for you to attach a clear story to the numbers you are seeing for Meta Platforms.

A Narrative is your own view of the company, written as a short story that sits behind the hard numbers like fair value, expected revenue, earnings and profit margins, so you are not just copying someone else’s model but spelling out what you believe and why.

Each Narrative links three things: what you think is happening in the business, what that implies for a financial forecast, and the fair value that drops out of those assumptions.

On Simply Wall St, Narratives live on the Community page and are designed to be easy to use. The platform is used by millions of investors, so you can quickly compare your view with others without needing to build a spreadsheet.

Because each Narrative includes a fair value, you can see at a glance whether your story says Meta looks expensive or offers value compared with the current share price. This can help you decide if it is closer to a buy, hold or sell for your own portfolio rules.

Narratives also update in real time when new information is added. If Meta reports earnings or there is fresh news about EU regulation or AI spending, the forecasts and fair values behind that story refresh automatically.

For Meta, one Narrative on Simply Wall St currently presents a fair value of about US$496.65 that leans heavily on regulatory risk in Europe and the possibility of a significant impact on the 23% of revenue that comes from that region. Another uses more optimistic revenue growth, margins near 39.1% and a future P/E of 26.9x to arrive at a fair value around US$850, showing how two investors can look at the same company and reach very different conclusions based on the story they believe.
