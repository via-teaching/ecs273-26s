Meta Platforms (NasdaqGS:META) has agreed a multi year deal with Amazon Web Services to deploy tens of millions of AWS Graviton5 CPU chips. The CPUs are intended to support next generation agentic AI workloads and reduce reliance on GPU only infrastructure. The agreement signals a shift in Meta's AI compute mix toward CPU heavy architectures and tighter collaboration with a major cloud provider. Meta Platforms comes into this move with its shares at $675.05 and a 23.7% return over the past year, alongside a 183.1% return over the past 3 years. In that context, investors have already seen substantial value creation as Meta has pushed deeper into AI and infrastructure build out. This AWS Graviton5 deal adds a new layer to Meta's AI approach, with a focus on large scale, CPU based agentic AI systems that could complement existing GPU clusters. Investors watching NasdaqGS:META may now pay closer attention to how this diversified compute stack affects Meta's cost profile, product roadmap, and its relationships across the semiconductor and cloud ecosystem. The AWS Graviton5 agreement points to Meta trying to secure a more flexible and diversified AI compute stack rather than relying purely on high end GPUs. Graviton CPUs are geared to handle large volumes of parallel, CPU intensive tasks such as orchestration, reasoning and multi step agentic AI workflows, which can complement GPU clusters that focus on model training and heavy inference. For you, this matters because compute mix affects Meta’s cost per AI task, how quickly it can roll out new AI features across Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, and how much dependency it has on any single chip or cloud supplier. Working closely with Amazon Web Services also adds another layer to Meta’s relationships with Nvidia, Broadcom and its own custom silicon efforts, and may change bargaining power over time as large tech peers such as Alphabet and Microsoft pursue their own AI chips. The large Graviton5 CPU deal supports the existing narrative that Meta is building multi gigawatt, AI focused infrastructure to drive long term monetization across ads, messaging and new AI services. Relying more on external cloud CPUs alongside custom chips and other partners adds execution complexity, which challenges the simpler narrative that AI infrastructure is mostly an in house, controlled asset base. The narrative focuses on AI driven engagement and revenue, but does not fully account for how a CPU heavy agentic architecture with AWS could influence Meta’s long term unit economics and supplier dependence.
