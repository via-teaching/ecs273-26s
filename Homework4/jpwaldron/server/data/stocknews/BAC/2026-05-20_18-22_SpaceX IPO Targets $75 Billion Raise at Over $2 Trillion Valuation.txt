Goldman Sachs (NYSE:GS) has reportedlysecuredthe leading role on the cover of SpaceX's initial public offering, giving the firm a prime position in what could become the largest listing ever. Morgan Stanley (NYSE:MS) is also listed as a lead bank, while Bank of America (NYSE:BAC), Citigroup (NYSE:C), and JPMorgan Chase (NYSE:JPM) appear in alphabetical order on the preliminary prospectus, according to people familiar with the matter. SpaceX, Elon Musk's rocket, satellite, and artificial intelligence company, is expected to file publicly for an IPO as soon as Wednesday, though the details remain under discussion and could still change. A bank's position on the cover of a major IPO can point to a more active role and, in some cases, a larger slice of the fees. SpaceX's planned offering is targeted to raise as much as $75 billion while seeking a valuation of more than $2 trillion, according to people familiar with the matter. That would put the deal far above Saudi Aramco's $29.4 billion IPO in 2019, based on Bloomberg calculations, making this a potentially defining mandate for the banks involved. The fee pool could be unusually large, with the listing expected to generate compensation well above a normal IPO. Citigroup was added to the banks leading the offering, joining Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, and JPMorgan, according to Bloomberg News. Beyond the core group, several other banks have reportedly been assigned regional roles: Barclays is handling UK IPO share orders, Deutsche Bank and UBS are working on European orders, Royal Bank of Canada is managing Canada orders, Mizuho Financial Group is focused on Asia orders, and Macquarie Group is covering Australia. For investors, the bigger signal is that SpaceX's possible market debut could become a major test of appetite for mega-scale private companies tied to space, satellites, and AI.
