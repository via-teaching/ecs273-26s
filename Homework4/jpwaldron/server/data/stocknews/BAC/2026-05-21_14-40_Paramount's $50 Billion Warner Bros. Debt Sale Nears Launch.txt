The investment-grade bond portion is expected to be sold in multiple parts, including three- and 10-year tranches, with early pricing discussions on the 10-year piece running 2.5 to 2.75 percentage points above the benchmark rate. The loan component is expected to be denominated in US dollars and euros, with the US loans being discussed at 3 percentage points above the benchmark rate and a discounted price of about 99 cents on the dollar. The high-yield bond portion is also expected to be split between US dollars and euros, with maturities of five, eight and 10 years, and with about $10 billion expected in US dollars and the rest in euros. The financing could help banks reduce balance-sheet risk after Apollo Global Management (NYSE:APO), Bank of America and Citigroup initially provided $57.5 billion of funding, later reduced to $49 billion and sold down to 18 banks in April. Bank of America is managing the junk-bond offering, Citigroup is running the loan sale, and both banks are leading the high-grade bond issue. The deal follows Paramount's $110 billion transaction for Warner Bros. Discovery, after Paramount beat Netflix (NASDAQ:NFLX) in a bidding war that could reshape the entertainment landscape.
