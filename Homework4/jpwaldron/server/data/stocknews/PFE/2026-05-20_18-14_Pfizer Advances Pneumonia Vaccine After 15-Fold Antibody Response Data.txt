Pfizer (NYSE:PFE) istryingto defend one of its most important vaccine franchises with a next-generation version of Prevnar, after early trial data suggested the experimental shot could give infants broader protection against common pneumonia-causing bacterial strains. The new vaccine targets 25 strains, five more than Prevnar 20, which has been approved for children since 2023. In a mid-stage trial, Pfizer said the shot generated strong immune responses in children up to 15 months old after four doses, including a 15-fold higher antibody response against serotype 3, a strain linked to more severe disease. Pfizer is now moving the vaccine into final-stage testing after presenting the data at the International Society of Pneumonia and Pneumococcal Diseases in Copenhagen. If the shot succeeds in those trials, the company said it could potentially protect against about 90% of disease-causing bacterial strains in children up to age five. Annaliesa Anderson, Pfizer's head of vaccine research and development, said the results beat the company's expectations and reflected Pfizer's continued commitment to vaccines. Side effects were described as mild and mostly in line with Prevnar 20, including injection-site pain and redness, while some infants experienced drowsiness and irritability, especially after the first dose. For investors, the update lands at a sensitive moment for Pfizer's vaccine business. Covid-19 shot demand has fallen sharply, making Prevnar and Pfizer's RSV vaccine for pregnant women more important to the company's vaccine portfolio. Prevnar generated about $6.5 billion in sales last year, making it one of Pfizer's top sellers, while Merck & Co. (NYSE:MRK) is pushing harder into the market after winning approval in 2024 for a new adult pneumonia vaccine with broader protection against severe infections. Pfizer is also preparing to test a 35-strain adult vaccine, covering 15 more bacterial strains than Prevnar 20, with trials expected to begin by the end of the year pending regulatory discussions.
