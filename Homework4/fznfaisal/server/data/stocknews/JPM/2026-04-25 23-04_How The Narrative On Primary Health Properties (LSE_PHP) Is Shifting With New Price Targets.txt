Both JPMorgan and Deutsche Bank have set formal price targets at £1.05, which aligns closely with the current £1.15 fair value estimate and signals continued interest in the shares at current levels.
JPMorgan’s move to a £1.05 target is framed around recent research work that, in their view, supports a higher ceiling than older assumptions. This gives you a clearer reference point when comparing market price to analyst expectations.
Deutsche Bank’s decision to lift its target by £0.01 to £1.05 reinforces that more than one major firm is now anchored around the same valuation area. Some investors may read this as a degree of consensus on PHPRF’s risk and reward trade off.
The step up in targets from both firms appears measured. While they see room between current pricing and £1.05, the implied upside signalled by this research is not extreme.
The clustering of targets around £1.05 suggests analysts see PHPRF within a relatively narrow valuation band, which may limit expectations for a wide re rating based purely on current information.
